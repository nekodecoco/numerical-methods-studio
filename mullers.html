Muller's Method Visualizer

By constructing a second-order parabola P(x) = a(x - x_2)^2 + b(x - x_2) + c through three points, Muller's method solves for its roots via a modified quadratic formula: x_3 = x_2 - 2c / (b ± √(b^2 - 4ac)). Remarkably, it converges with near-quadratic speed (p ≈ 1.84) and can locate complex conjugate roots even from real starting guesses!

💡 Parabolic Interpolation & Complex Roots

Parabolic vs. Linear: Straight lines cannot capture function curvature. By using three points to solve for coefficients a, b, c, Muller's method bends along the function contour, accelerating convergence order to p ≈ 1.84.

Complex Roots: If b² - 4ac < 0, the root has an imaginary part i√(4ac - b²). This allows Muller's method to find complex roots of polynomials that cannot be reached by Bisection or False Position!
